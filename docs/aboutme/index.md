#About Me

###Matthew Cowan

I am a mechanical engineering student at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte. I am currently developing the technical skills needed to solve practical engineering problems through analysis, design, and testing. My interest in mechanical engineering comes from an interest in understanding how mechanical systems work and how their performance can be improved through better design. As a child growing up I would take apart engines and put different parts into them in an attempt to make them perform more efficiently and produce more horsepower and torque, which I am now learning the design ideas behind. I am also intrigued by applications where engineering decisions have a direct effect on the strength, reliability, efficiency, and performance of a system.  I am currently developing an engineering centered approach that combines theoretical knowledge with practical applications. I want to become an engineer that would be able to take a problem, identify the important variables as well as constraints, develop a solution, and support that solution with calculations. Rather than relying on whether a design works or not, I am learning to understand the ideas behind why they work and what is necessary to make a valid analysis. As I continue through mechanical engineering, I want to strengthen my skills in mechanical design, manufacturing, analysis, and technical communication. My goal is to become a mechanical engineer with a concentration in motorsports and become the type of engineer to diagnose an issue with an engine, find a solution to either fix or improve the problem that is currently being presented, and fix the problem with long lasting improvements and durability that will allow for future reinforcements in other areas to make the overall engine stronger and more powerful as well as helping my coworkers and not hindering their ability to create well thought out designs. 

To defend an engineering decision means explaining why a choice was made using requirements, evidence, calculations, and comparisons to other options instead of just saying that it works. I understand the basic idea, but I am still working on supporting my decisions with enough technical evidence. I can explain why I would choose one design over another, but I need more practice using measurable criteria and engineering analysis to support those choices. This semester should help me improve by requiring me to explain and justify my design decisions instead of only showing the final result.
